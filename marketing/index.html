Every ad is judged on the result Meta was told to buy. How this board decides
  • Verdict uses the campaign's own objective (see Ad type). A landing page view campaign is judged on cost per LPV, not bookings.
  • Bookings show on every row, so cheap traffic is never mistaken for a full restaurant.
  • CPA is £ per booking, order or lead on every row; the objective cost sits under it. CPC is £ per link click. Owner: Vidya.
  • CTR, LPV and ThruPlay % (grey band) are background only and decide nothing. Low CTR: ad not pulling (Georgie). Low LPV: landing page losing people (Vidya). Low ThruPlay: video loses people early (Georgie).
  • Kills stay at the top until switched off. Once stopped they move to decision history. Click any row for its history.

Tiers, re-verified from source

Recomputed, zero mismatch

Checked against the member sheet rather than the summary tab, and independently recomputed from the points columns. The stored tier and the formula agree on every row. The tier bands are right. What is wrong is underneath them.

Tier distribution all members

TierThresholdMembersEx-staffShare

Two of the seven Royalty members are labelled "Ramen Royalty", not "Royalty". Any filter written as tier = 'Royalty' silently loses them, which is exactly what the summary tab was doing.

Three faults under the tier

  • 1The top tiers have collapsed since 26 August. Noble was 588 and is now 337. Elite was 236 and is now 85. Tier is banded on the live points balance, so redeeming points demotes a member instantly, and lifetime_points_spent is zero on every row, so nothing records that they ever earned it. A member is being quietly demoted for using the scheme.
  • 2Nothing anywhere records tier history. No snapshot table, no tier-change event, no dated log. "How many people moved up today" cannot be answered, only inferred from reward unlocks. This is the single cheapest fix on the list.
  • 3One seed row holds 100,000,000 points (teddy@makiramen.com, inactive) and sits in Royalty. It distorts every points total by roughly £1m on the /100 basis.

Reaching the rungs

Points ladder

Every threshold crossed fires its own reward email, so recipients are a clean count of progression. Engagement climbs steeply with prize value, which says the ladder is designed correctly and almost nobody gets high enough to enjoy it.

ThresholdRewardReached itClaimedRedeemedEmail clickFunnel from unlock

Redemption rate rises fourteen-fold up the ladder (2.6% at the free drink, 26.5% at the free ramen). The bottom rung is the one almost everyone gets and almost nobody uses. A free drink is not worth opening the app for. Free Ramen Bowl shows 15 claims against 1 unlock, so the 1,000-point reward is reaching people who did not earn it. Worth a look before it is promoted.

Sign-ups in context

New sign-upsSelected day

How to read a sign-up number. A day that has not finished shows a running total, not the day's total. On 12 September it stood near 500 at 2pm and closed at 776, so the same dashboard read at lunchtime and at midnight gives two different, both correct, answers. The day view stamps the live day with the time its figure was taken: quote the stamp with the number, or quote a completed day.

Basis. new_members on DAILY METRICS, which counts Trifft registrations by registered_at. Klaviyo's Member verified series runs a few per cent lower because it counts only registrations that verified (12 September: 776 here, 750 there). Both are defensible; they are not a contradiction. Never mix the two in one sentence, and name the source when the number goes in front of anyone.

New sign-ups split per site. Not possible today. Only 29.2% of people who have joined since go-live carry a venue in any field, and that venue comes from a later transaction rather than the sign-up itself.
