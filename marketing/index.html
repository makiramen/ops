V0

Expiry day: the deadline pulled

The 36,265-voucher batch died at 23:59 on 1 September. Nobody was told it was going to. The catch-up campaign was never scheduled, the automated nudge flow has still never sent a single email, and there is no SMS in the account. This is what the deadline did entirely on its own.

Redeemed per day

One day out of fifteen carried 19% of every redemption since go-live.

Claimed per day

Claims doubled as well, so this is people opening the app and finding the voucher — not only spending one already in hand.

Why this counts as evidence rather than noise. No message went out, so there is nothing to attribute the lift to except the expiry itself. And the V2 baseline below measures the days inside the window and finds no end-of-window drop-off at all — so a spike on the deadline cannot be normal behaviour showing through. If an untold deadline doubles the day, a told one has room above it. This is the number the mechanic in V1 has to be judged against.

V1

The new expiry mechanic

Agreed 1 Sept, not yet live

The voucher lives 14 days and is advertised as 7. On day 7 the member who has not used it gets an extension framed as a favour. On day 12 they get one last push, two days before it actually dies. The baseline in V2 measures the days inside the window and finds no drop-off approaching day 14 — which is what makes V0 above readable: when the deadline itself landed on 1 September it produced the biggest day the programme has had.

Half of this is already in the product. The £5 coupon's own description field reads "valid for 7 days from the date it goes live on customers account" while the real window is 14 days. The advertised seven already exists. Nothing has ever acted on it, and redemptions run out to day 13 with no drop-off at day 7 at all.

V2

The baseline it has to beat

Days between a member being given the £5 and using it, measured from their own clock rather than the calendar. The two cohorts are almost exactly half each and behave like different species, so they are never pooled here.

Migrated base, 18 Aug batch

Flat across the whole window. They redeem whenever they happen to come in, not because a deadline is approaching.

Post-launch sign-ups

73.5% redeem on day zero and 95.5% are done by day six. They are standing in a restaurant with the app open. No expiry mechanic will move these people.

Where redemptions land

WindowMigratedNew

The highlighted rows are the two the mechanic targets. On the migrated base that is 54% of all redemptions already happening in the extension window, unprompted.

Does the deadline pull anything today?

Day of windowHazard rate

No. The end of the window is quieter than the middle. Redemptions as a share of the still-unredeemed pool fall as the deadline approaches. That is the case for the mechanic in one table: there is no existing deadline effect to confuse it with.

How to read the result

  • D7Any spike on days 7 and 8 is the extension nudge. Baseline is 58 and 44 redemptions.
  • D12Any spike on days 12 and 13 is the final push. Baseline is 66 and 57.
  • D0-6Movement here would have happened anyway. Do not claim it.

Report the two cohorts separately every time. Pooling them hides the effect entirely, because new sign-ups swamp day zero.

V3

Before it ships

Check what the app wallet shows. Trifft holds the real 14-day date on the coupon as expiration_at, and the wallet is where the member goes to claim. If it renders that date, the email's seven days is contradicted on screen and the day-7 extension reads as a lie rather than a gift. This is the one thing that can kill the mechanic, and it takes five minutes to check.
The clean version, if the wallet does show it. Set the coupon to a real 7 days, and on day 7 issue a fresh 7-day coupon to anyone who has not redeemed. It is then a genuine extension, nothing on screen contradicts anything, and the re-issue fires its own Coupon assigned event, which hands you the tracked cohort for free with no segment maths at all. This is the recommendation.

Tracking needs no new plumbing either way. Both the issue and the redemption already carry a timestamp and a profile id, so days-to-redeem is computable per person from events that exist today. One caution on the audience: use a between-static date window, never "in the last N days", which is the bug that pointed the catch-up campaign at 2,322 recent sign-ups instead of the cliff.

The baseline is 70% complete on day 13 and blind on day 14. At the data cut the migrated cohort was 13.7 days old, so nobody had finished a full window. Re-run after 2 September to lock the numbers before the change goes live.

    Section 2 · What drives recommendation

    Fix the bowl before the smile

    The question this section answers

    Every customer scored three things out of 10: the food on their last visit, the service on that visit, and how likely they are to recommend us. So we can ask the operationally useful question: if only one of the two is bad, which one costs us the customer?

    How we measure "recommend"

    Anyone who scores 9 or 10 out of 10 is a promoter (they would actively recommend us). 7 or 8 is passive. 0 to 6 is a detractor. Across all 1,210 people: 70% promoters, 25% passive, 5% detractors.

    A note on NPS

    NPS is just promoters minus detractors, so 70 − 5 = +66. It runs from −100 to +100 and it is not a percentage of anything. It is on this page because the business reports it, but everything below is in plain percentages instead.

    Floor  Where the fix is the service

    Leicester, Leeds, Soho, Southampton, Lakeside, St James Quarter. Leicester first, and it is getting worse: 48% had a poor visit (44% at read-out 2), more than double the estate. Leeds is the biggest base with a service problem (8.3 on 96), and its comments are about complaints not answered, which is a management response problem.

    When "social media" is the top fix (Bath Street, Renfield Street, Metrocentre) it means nothing at the restaurant needs fixing: those are our best-run sites, and their customers move on to asking for better marketing instead. That is not a shrug, it is a real ask, and section 9 shows it is joint number one across the whole estate.

    What it means

    The estate does not have a service problem. One region does, and inside it the fix is different site by site. Leicester is now both the lowest NPS and the highest poor-visit rate in the business, and it has moved the wrong way in three days. Visit it this week. Put the five kitchen sites on a food consistency programme. Both are North England & Midlands AM actions.
