The deadline, worked backwards

Effective date of termination, not decision date. The new regime applies where the EDT falls on or after 1 January 2027 — and that includes dismissals decided earlier where statutory notice pushes the EDT past the cutoff. To land an EDT on or before 31 December 2026 with one month's notice, notice must be served by 30 November. A fair capability process under the ACAS Code needs roughly eight weeks end to end: standard set, structured support, formal meeting with advance written notice, written outcome, genuine right of appeal. Work back from 30 November and the last date to open a formal capability process for a 2026 EDT is 30 September 2026.

And the thing that matters more than the deadline: you cannot dismiss someone to get ahead of the date. The commencement rules address that directly, and a compressed process is exactly what a tribunal reads as unfairness. The right response to 1 January is not to accelerate exits — it is to stop letting capability decisions drift, run each one properly from now, and accept that anything unfinished in December is run under the new regime with full documentation. None of this is legal advice. Hanna Awang confirms every case and employment law advice is taken on anything contested.

What this pack does not do

It does not set pay. It produces the evidence pay decisions are taken from, at the group's own cadence. It does not replace the GM Performance Tracker — the monthly scores roll into the same four dimensions and the same weights, so the two are one system. And it does not decide who leaves: it makes each decision defensible, or shows honestly that it is not.

The ruling: the Area Manager owns B2B in region, end to end. Target list, contact, meeting, close, delivery, revenue. Not a marketing hand-off. Reporting, sign-off and close-out are held in the region, not centrally. That is Charter v2.5 Pillar 5, and it is the pillar with the least evidence behind it today.

Why the current model produced nothing

London ran it first and the result is the cleanest evidence in the business: 120 Shoreditch targets, 36 contacted, 0 deals, £0 revenue, and a 350-target London list dormant since April. The cause was diagnosed at the time and it was not effort. Outreach started before the foundations existed. No priced catering menu was filed anywhere the team could reach. Slerp was switched off across the estate on 27 July so there was no ordering channel. Deliveroo platform permissions were unconfirmed. And tasting drops — the core mechanic of the whole plan — never happened once: every Wave 1 target on the doorstep was still Not Started, and the team emailed hotels instead.

Activity without revenue does not count, and activity without foundations does not even produce activity. The one thing that did work is the counter-example worth copying: G4 Padel, a named strategic partner, £1,000 committed across two phases, 300 loyalty members and £3,000 of revenue. One properly-founded partnership out-earned 120 cold targets by infinity.

The four success measures

Everything else is noise. These four convert to a single monthly RAG per region.

Contact rate

Contacted ÷ qualified

Standard: 100% of Wave 1 within 30 days of the list going live. This is the measure London failed first — 36 of 120 contacted, and the 84 were never worked. Contact rate below 80% means the list is a document, not a pipeline.

Meeting rate

Meetings held ÷ contacted

Standard: ≥20%. If contact is high and meetings are low, the approach is wrong — almost always because the contact was an email rather than a tasting drop on foot. Fix the mechanic, not the volume.

Close rate

Confirmed ÷ meetings held

Standard: ≥33%. One in three meetings should convert when the four foundations are in place. A low close rate with good meetings usually means no priced menu or no ordering channel — that is F1 and F2, not the AM.

Revenue per account

Banked ÷ confirmed accounts

Standard: ≥£1,500 annualised. Guards against a pipeline padded with tiny accounts to hit a count. Twenty £200 accounts is not eight real ones.

The overriding guard: 100% activity with £0 revenue is a fail, not an amber. If a region hits every activity floor for two consecutive months and has banked nothing, the AM stops adding targets and the Ops Director reviews the foundations. The problem will be F1 to F4, and adding more cold contacts will not touch it.

The timing rule, stated plainly

  1. EDT on or before 31 December 2026
    The termination must take effect in 2026, not merely be decided in 2026. Statutory notice counts towards the EDT.
  2. Therefore notice served by 30 November — on a one-month notice period
    Longer contractual notice moves this earlier, proportionally. This is the number to confirm with HR before anything else on the page.
  3. Therefore a formal process opened by 30 September
    A fair capability process under the ACAS Code needs roughly eight weeks: standard set in writing, structured support, formal meeting with advance written notice of the concerns, right to be accompanied, written outcome, and a genuine right of appeal with time to use it.
  4. And the warning that matters more than the deadline
    You cannot dismiss someone to get ahead of the date. The commencement position addresses that directly, and a compressed process is precisely what a tribunal reads as unfairness — the 25% uplift for failing the Code applies on top of an uncapped award. The correct response to 1 January is not to accelerate exits. It is to stop letting capability decisions drift, run each one properly from now, and accept that anything unfinished in December runs under the new regime with full documentation.

What a compliant plan contains

The same shape for every case, capability or conduct. If any line is missing the plan is not evidence, it is correspondence.

  1. 1 · The standard, in writing, dated and signed
    Charter Day 0: wage 21%, food 25%, covers, rating, compliance, team trained. What good looks like for this role at this site. Gate G1. None of the four has one on file today, which is why the first two weeks of the runway are evidence-building, not meetings.
  2. 2 · The gap, with the evidence beside it
    Each shortfall stated against the standard, with a dated source: the Q3 form, the Q2 tracker, the live KPI week, the turnover line, the review text. Never a summary without a source. Never an inference presented as a fact.
  3. 3 · What is not being charged to them
    Written down explicitly. The extraction fan at M11. Kitchen spec at M11. The Byres Road opening at M6. This is the section that protects the plan, and the one most often left out.
  4. 4 · Three or fewer measurable outcomes, each with a number and a date
    "Food cost at or under 25% for four consecutive weeks" is measurable. "Progression to do better" and "continuously" are not. Every one of the four current plans fails this test as written.
  5. 5 · The support being given, and by whom
    Gate G3: the coaching, the training, the tool that was genuinely missing, with weekly notes across the support window. The AM has to be able to show what was removed from the path, not just what was demanded.
  6. 6 · Defined review points, and a defined end date
    Formal review at week 4 and week 8, with a stated final review date. Not "ongoing". Six of the eleven AGM deadlines in this round are open-ended and would be unenforceable.
  7. 7 · The consequence, stated plainly
    What happens if the standard is not met. If it is not stated at the start, it cannot be applied at the end.
  8. 8 · Process rights on every invitation
    Gate G5: written notice of the meeting and the concerns in advance, right to be accompanied, written outcome with reasons, genuine right of appeal heard by someone not involved in the decision.
  9. 9 · HR eligibility confirmed in writing before the first meeting is booked
    Gate G4: continuous service today and at the projected EDT, contractual notice period, any live grievance. Hanna Awang, case by case, in writing.
