What was double counted

Modifier lines. Kobas prints "of which ..." rows that sit inside their parent item. They added 267,111 units and £274k of net sales that were already counted. Out, and the report's own total then agrees to the penny.

Duplicate till buttons. The same dish is rung through under a year tag, a delivery tag, a brand or site tag or a promo tag. 132 till names fold into 62 real items.

Left alone on purpose. Halal, vegan and vegetarian versions, frozen retail lines, drink sizes, and the £5 / £8 / Lunch promo buttons. Different products, not duplicates.

Can We Afford the Peg?

Five questions, five answers, one slider. Every figure comes from our own management accounts, our own Kobas rota and our own till data. Nothing here is an industry benchmark.

What it costs
−£558k
Net profit a year, on the eight sites with a full set of 2025 accounts. That is 2.66 points of margin.
What it takes back
+21
Extra covers per site, per day, every day, forever. Not a campaign: a permanent step change in trade.
What it does not touch
£2.8m
Cost increases already coming over two years. The peg hedges wages and nothing else.
1

What does the peg cost us?

The April 2027 wage rise happens either way and costs us 0.74 points. The peg costs a further 2.66 points on top, and that is the part we choose.

The honest comparison is the second bar against the third. Doing nothing still costs us margin, because the wage floor rises whatever we do. The peg's own price is £558,050 and 2.66 points on these eight sites. Scaled across the 18 corporate sites at the July 2026 run rate, the day-one revenue cut is £1.37m a year.

2

What do we actually pay the team?

Under the law alone, our newest kitchen assistant gets a rise and our kitchen supervisor gets nothing. Holding the whole ladder instead costs £68k a year, which is two tenths of one per cent of sales.

Run on every individual rate rather than the grade median, the group totals are higher (about £181k statutory and £242k on a hold-the-ladder policy for April 2027) because roughly a third of staff sit below their grade median. The gap between the two policies is what the decision turns on, and it is the same shape either way. General Manager (£36,500), Assistant Manager (£32,000) and Head Chef (£36,000) are salaried and held flat throughout: 19.5% of hours and 20.8% of the wage bill, insulated by construction. One thing to diary: a £32,000 AGM breaches the floor above 46.7 hours a week in April 2027 and 45.2 in April 2028, and one AGM already records 46.7.

What else is going up?

Over two years our cost base rises £2.83m. The peg's own price rises give back £835k. It covers under a third of the problem and opens a £1.37m hole on day one to do it.

5

So what do we do?

Two real choices. Doing nothing still costs us 0.74 points to the April 2027 wage rise. The peg costs 3.40 points and puts £1.67m of revenue on the table for a cover uplift we have no evidence we can win.

Option A

Do nothing

Reprice the ramen page at menu inflation like everyone else. No story, no risk, and we still lose margin to the April 2027 wage rise.

Peg the whole page

Every bowl down 12.34% to £12.71, Steak Tataki held. The cleanest version of the idea and by far the most expensive.

+21 covers a site a day is the test Option B has to pass. No UK ramen chain prices a full-size bowl under an hour of minimum wage on its standard menu, so there is no precedent to size the uplift from. What evidence there is points the other way: UK restaurant transactions fell 1.3% year on year in August 2026 while spend rose 1.5%, and Berkeley's study of California's wage floor found an 8% to 9% wage shock moved menu prices only 1.5%. The uplift has to come from new trial, not from existing guests coming more often, because every cover that was coming anyway takes the discount and gives nothing back.

The calendar decides this for us. The Low Pay Commission reports by the end of October and the April 2027 rate lands at the Autumn Budget on 28 October, two weeks before the menu architecture locks on 13 November and five weeks before pricing closes on 4 December. We will know the real number before we have to commit to it. One decision is worth taking on its own merits regardless of the peg: hold the hourly ladder for £68k a year.
